# Writing Academy pedagogical standards

Use these standards for Review Writing, Essay Writing and Informal Email. Define the writing decision a student should make independently before designing its activity.

| Stage | Purpose | Student action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Discover | Understand a technique | Notice a change between authentic examples and explain its effect before revealing the principle. |
| Explore | Analyse a model in context | Justify an editorial decision in writing, then examine the model and sentence annotations. |
| Practise | Build control at increasing levels of independence | Use Bronze to recognise or sequence with scaffolding, Silver to discriminate and diagnose, and Gold to produce or substantively edit. |
| Apply | Use the technique in a realistic short workshop | Draft independently, compare, reflect against criteria and revise. |
| Master | Transfer the technique to a fresh context | Make an analytical decision, produce or edit writing with reduced scaffolding, and reflect before moving to a full Studio task. |

## Questions and feedback

- State the specific criterion and provide the context needed to apply it. A useful comparison might ask which edit preserves the scope of a criticism about noise at lunchtime. Compare realistic edits that preserve the timing, broaden it, or intensify the criticism.
- Use plausible student alternatives that represent recognisable errors. A criticism of a library should never sit beside unrelated choices about a meal or a football game. Keep alternatives comparable in length, fluency and register unless one of those features is the skill being assessed.
- Treat a defensible alternative as an opportunity for discussion or a constructed response. Sequence full sentences only when references or logic give a defensible order. Use accessible keyboard controls and retain underlying option identities when randomising their display.
- Explain what each alternative does and why that matters. For sequencing, explain the links that constrain the order. Avoid feedback that names answer letters because rendering may reorder choices.
- Review every item for unintended clues. Pay particular attention to copied phrases, predictable positions, formatting, and a correct answer that is consistently longer or more detailed.

## Independent writing

Supply a realistic audience, purpose, observations and a clear response length. Editing tasks must preserve supplied facts. Original composition may invite students to supply their own plausible details. Write an original model that meets the same constraints as the student’s response.

Require an independent attempt before sharing one possible response. Preserve the first draft while the student revises. Use a short checklist about the writing skill, such as whether a recommendation follows the evidence. Check completion through student reflection and revision. A minimum-length check is an effort gate. Automatically scored decisions, self-assessment and any externally reviewed feedback each communicate different kinds of evidence.

## Curriculum and architecture

Keep exam facts in the shared verified reference and confirm any future changes against official materials for the intended series. Design for task fulfilment, relevant ideas, development, audience, register, organisation, range and accuracy. Treat Academy levels and genre scaffolds as teaching tools. Keep AI optional and use only the existing Studio workflow within its access and quota rules.

Keep writing content separate from scoring, rendering and state. Use shared activity components with stable IDs and option mappings. Support keyboard interaction and maintain draft, revision and checklist state when changing stages during a visit. Reuse model text and attach annotations to the relevant sentences.

## Content and release checklist

- Review every task against its intended skill, audience, level and transfer value.
- Check that every key is defensible, alternatives are credible, and feedback explains the alternatives.
- Check IDs, fields, shuffled mappings, model annotations, duplicate options or prompts, and unusually disproportionate lengths. Treat heuristic flags as review prompts.
- Read each model against the supplied facts and verify that its checklist supports different effective responses.
- Test navigation, completion, keyboard use, answer submission, retries, delayed reveal and revision on desktop, tablet and mobile.
- Record test coverage and any release limitations, including the difference between a simulated session and authenticated production behaviour.
