## Instructional-language review

- **Discover.** The learner reads two examples and makes one decision. The lines are labelled First and Second. “Check answer” acts on a deliberate choice. “Try another answer” leaves the learner in the question.
- **Explore.** “Paragraph” and “review” are course vocabulary. The model picker retains titles and categories. Paragraph numbers give position without identifying a function. A sentence note is accessible by mouse or keyboard. The question itself does not require an editor’s note.
- **Practise.** Challenge titles describe the action. The ordering guidance uses events, causes or advice rather than naming a grammatical relation students must decode. Feedback explains why details belong together. The objective score counts correct decisions.
- **Gold, Apply and Master.** Prompts specify audience and output. Longer instructions use steps. Checklists use first-person statements. “Compare with an example” follows the first draft and the review panel identifies self-assessment. Word limits keep workshops moving and do not grade quality.
- **Progress.** First try, retry and support have different labels. The stage count tracks completion. Explore retains the previous requirement to finish one model. Teacher judgement is needed on whether later class assignments should require more than one model and whether the second failed answer is the right point to reveal an explanation.
- **Readability check.** Stems request one decision. Some scenarios and model paragraphs intentionally carry exam-level vocabulary. The meaning of terms such as “dialogue”, “caption”, “recommendation”, “refillable” and “admission” may merit observation during a student trial. No learner trial is recorded here.
