# Reconstruction batch 01 — construct-first replacement candidates (v2)

**Status:** Proposed editorial designs only; no database writes, no approvals, no CEFR certification. This second pass replaces the earlier “select the sensible outcome” approach with **lexical selection in context**. Correct options are target words or target phrases; distractors are same grammatical class and close enough to require reading the context. Every item needs independent key review and empirical calibration.

**Design trade-off:** Putting the target word among options measures *recognition and selection*, not productive vocabulary. A mixed diagnostic should eventually include additional receptive formats and independently calibrated items. Keys are not distributed evenly by force; avoid position clues through randomisation in delivery.

## A1 / 003 — choose
**Stem:** There are three clubs, but I can join only one. I need to ___ which one I like best.
- A. describe
- **B. choose (key)**
- C. visit
- D. explain

**Key rationale:** *choose* means select from alternatives. *Describe*, *visit* and *explain* could all relate to clubs but do not express selecting one. **Risk:** “only one” is a direct semantic cue; intended as an easier anchor, not a high-discrimination item.

## A1 / 004 — price
**Stem:** “It was £8 yesterday, but now it costs £10.” “So the ___ has gone up.”
- A. size
- B. number
- C. value
- **D. price (key)**

**Key rationale:** The amount charged for an item is its *price*. *Value* is a **potential second answer in informal usage**; **HOLD — NOT APPROVABLE** until C is replaced by an equally plausible but unambiguously wrong noun. A reviewer should test alternatives such as *discount*, though that too can be affected by price changes.

## A2 / 015 — invite
**Stem:** We are having a school information evening on Thursday. We would like to ___ all parents to attend.
- A. remind
- B. inform
- **C. invite (key)**
- D. advise

**Key rationale:** *Invite someone to attend* is a conventional collocation for asking them to come. *Remind* implies a prior arrangement; *inform* and *advise* do not themselves request attendance. **Risk:** May be solved through collocation knowledge rather than deeper lexical meaning; this is acceptable only if the construct explicitly includes lexical collocations.

## A2 / 018 — remind
**Stem:** I know you have already agreed to bring the books, but I wanted to ___ you before tomorrow.
- A. invite
- B. warn
- C. explain
- **D. remind (key)**

**Key rationale:** *Remind* brings a previously known obligation back to mind. *Warn* can be used before tomorrow but implies danger; *invite* implies a request to attend; *explain* takes a different object pattern here. **Risk:** *Explain* is partly eliminated by grammar; seek a grammatically parallel fourth distractor (e.g. *contact*) in independent review.

## B1 / 022 — consequence
**Stem:** The school shortened lunch breaks. One unexpected ___ was that fewer students attended lunchtime clubs.
- A. intention
- B. proposal
- C. requirement
- **D. consequence (key)**

**Key rationale:** A *consequence* is a result of an action; the other nouns describe motives, suggestions or obligations. **Risk:** *Unexpected* plus a resulting event strongly cues *consequence*, but no longer gives a direct synonym.

## B2 / 070 — adequate
**Stem:** The equipment is not the best available, but it is ___ for the basic tasks we need to complete.
- **A. adequate (key)**
- B. exceptional
- C. excessive
- D. unsuitable

**Key rationale:** *Adequate* means sufficient for the purpose, even if not excellent. **Risk:** *Exceptional*, *excessive* and *unsuitable* are quite far apart in meaning; revise distractors with level-matched near alternatives before approval.

## C1 / 044 — nuanced
**Stem:** The researcher avoided calling the programme simply a success or a failure. Instead, she offered a ___ assessment that considered differences between student groups.
- A. decisive
- B. uniform
- **C. nuanced (key)**
- D. superficial

**Key rationale:** *Nuanced* means attentive to subtle distinctions; *decisive* can be strong but not necessarily detailed, *uniform* ignores differences, *superficial* lacks depth. **Risk:** *Instead* and explicit “differences” signal the key; review difficulty and whether the answer is too easy for C1.

## B2 / 088 — counterproductive
**Stem:** The extra reminders were meant to improve homework submission, but students began ignoring every notification. In the end, the policy proved ___.
- **A. counterproductive (key)**
- B. ineffective
- C. unnecessary
- D. unpopular

**Key rationale:** The intended intervention worsened the problem. **CRITICAL AMBIGUITY:** The stem does not actually say late submissions increased. *Ineffective* is also defensible, and *unpopular* may be inferred. **HOLD — NOT APPROVABLE.** Restore explicit evidence that lateness worsened, then test whether *ineffective* remains an acceptable alternative (it may still be a true, but less precise, description).

## Release decision for batch 01

- **Never** treat a proposed v2 as a verified replacement just because it has one designated key.
- Items 004 and 088 are explicitly blocked for ambiguous keys; others require independent checks for distractor quality and construct alignment.
- A future student diagnostic needs an item blueprint: balanced receptive tasks, tested distractors, sensible coverage across levels and pilot-based item discrimination.
- Retain v1 alongside v2 for comparison; do not update the live draft bank or advance any review stage automatically.
